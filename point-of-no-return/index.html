The point
of no return

When does a quantum possibility stop being undoable? In a laboratory in Fuzhou, a microwave cat state came back from the dead — until its environment grew from one qubit to eight, and the way back closed. In a model of the infant universe, the same logic locks a whole cosmos into its vacuum. Irreversibility, it turns out, is a coordination problem.

Chapter 1

The cat that came back — until it didn't

Zheng and colleagues stored a photonic "cat state" — a microwave field in a superposition of empty and light-filled — in a superconducting resonator, and coupled it to an artificial environment they could grow one qubit at a time.

Their measure was distinguishability: how well the environment, read as a whole, could tell the cat's two components apart. Zero means the environment knows nothing and interference survives; one means the which-path record is complete and the interference is gone.

With a single qubit as the environment, the record faded and then un-wrote itself: around 38 nanoseconds in, the qubit nearly completed an oscillation and the interference nearly revived. With eight qubits, the distinguishability approached one after about 15 nanoseconds and stayed there through measurements out to 80 ns — and Wigner maps showed the interference fading between 0 and 30 ns without coming back.

Chapter 2

The coordination problem

Why does adding qubits close the way back? Because erasure is a group project, and the group never agrees.

Each environmental qubit exchanges energy with the microwave field at its own rate. To erase the environment's combined record, every qubit has to return, at the same time, to a state that no longer distinguishes the cat's two components. With one qubit, that return is a single oscillation — it happens. With eight qubits running at different rates, the joint return is a coincidence that gets rarer the larger the group grows.

That is the whole mechanism of irreversibility in this experiment: not a law that forbids return, but a coordination problem that makes return vanishingly unlikely. The information isn't destroyed. It's distributed — and a distributed record can't be un-written by any single member coming home.

Irreversibility is not a wall. It is a crowd — too many witnesses, each keeping a slightly different time, for the record ever to be un-made.

Chapter 3

The lockdown

Now scale the same logic up — from a resonator to a universe. Christie, Joo, Kaplanek, Vennin and Wands asked what decoherence does to a quantum field choosing between vacua during inflation.

Picture a landscape of valleys: the true vacuum is the deepest, and the shallower ones are false vacua. A field can sit in a false valley while a deeper one exists nearby, and quantum mechanics would ordinarily let it tunnel across. But the field is never alone — it is surrounded by spectator fields, an environment that continuously gathers information about which valley it is in.

The model's result has two parts. First, where the field lands is mostly decided before decoherence enters: a field heavy compared with the Hubble scale — the expansion rate — relaxes adiabatically toward the true vacuum with high probability, while a light field, unable to keep up with the expansion, can end up with significant probability in the false vacuum. Second, once it has landed, decoherence keeps it there: tunneling between vacua is strongly suppressed, a "cosmic lockdown" the authors read as a manifestation of the quantum Zeno effect — continuous environmental monitoring freezing the field in place.

The subtle point, and the one Kaplanek stresses: the environment does not choose. It keeps. The choice is made by the race between the field's own dynamics and the expansion; the lock clicks shut afterwards.

Adjusts quickly as the universe expands, tracks the changing landscape, and relaxes toward the true vacuum with high probability. The lockdown then guards a good outcome.

Light field (mass ≪ Hubble scale)

Cannot keep up with the expansion; a significant probability remains of ending in the false vacuum. The lockdown then guards a bad one — and that, the authors note, is how a universe could get stuck in a valley that isn't the bottom.

About the Higgs: some calculations suggest our own Higgs field may rest in a false vacuum. This model does not settle that question — it is a simplified setup, a scalar field in an asymmetric double well during inflation, not the Standard Model. What it does say is that if a field lands in a false vacuum in an environment like this, decoherence is a reason it might stay there far longer than naive tunneling rates suggest. Suppressed, the authors stress, is not impossible.

Robson Christie, Jaewoo Joo, Greg Kaplanek, Vincent Vennin & David Wands, "Cosmic Lockdown: When Decoherence Saves the Universe from Tunneling," Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics (2026); arXiv:2512.14204. Coverage: phys.org, "Cosmic lockdown: How the environment can isolate quantum fields" (Sept 2026); ScienceAlert, "The Universe Seems to Be Stuck in Place by a Strange Quantum Phenomenon."

Chapter 4

Kill conditions

A drawer about irreversibility should say what would undo it.

If the 8-qubit revival shows up late

Zheng's window ran to 80 ns for distinguishability and 0–30 ns for the Wigner revival check. A revival at, say, 200 ns would not falsify the experiment — it would reframe the headline from "irreversible" to "delayed," and the coordination story would need a timescale, not just a headcount.

If lockdown breaks in a fuller model

Christie's setup is a simplified model: one scalar field, spectator-field environment, no backreaction on the expansion. If a model with backreaction, or with the actual Higgs potential, restores efficient tunneling, the lockdown becomes a toy mechanism — instructive about decoherence, not a fact about our universe.

If erasure stops being a coordination problem

The Fuzhou mechanism leans on qubits running at different rates. An environment engineered so its members return together — synchronized couplings, a shared clock — would be a direct test: if the revival returns with many qubits, coordination was the whole story.

If the Higgs question closes elsewhere

Precision measurements of the Higgs mass and the top quark mass could, in principle, settle whether our vacuum is false at all. If the answer comes back "true vacuum," the lockdown's stakes for our universe dissolve — while its lesson about decoherence stands.

Coda

What persists is what got witnessed

Put the two papers side by side and a shape appears. In the resonator, a record kept by one witness can be un-kept; a record kept by eight cannot, for all practical purposes. In the infant universe, a valley chosen by a race gets guarded by an audience. In both cases, the past hardens not when something is destroyed but when something is distributed widely enough.

That reframes a familiar mystery. The quantum-to-classical transition is often told as a story about loss — coherence leaking away. These two papers tell it as a story about keeping: the environment keeps a copy, and a copy kept by enough witnesses is what we call a fact. Irreversibility is what a record looks like once it has too many custodians to recall.

The universe does not forget by erasing. It forgets by remembering too widely to take back.
