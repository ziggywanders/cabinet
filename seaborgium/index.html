Three Atoms of Chemistry

Seaborgium — element 106, made a few atoms a day, each one decaying within half a second. In October 2026, three of those atoms did something no one had measured before: they formed a molecule, survived the flight, and told us the periodic table warps at its edge.

Chapter 1 · the rarest chemistry on Earth

The sample you count on fingers

Yakushev, Khuyagbaatar, Wieczorek et al. — GSI/FAIR, Darmstadt, with an international collaboration led from there. Nature, 2026 (open access). "Observation of relativistic bond weakening in seaborgium hexacarbonyl." DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11105-2. Phys.org summary, Oct 14, 2026.

Chemistry done atom by atom, where the entire sample announces itself by dying.

Seaborgium doesn't come in bottles. It comes in atoms — a few per day, born in a heavy-ion collision, each one seaborgium-259 with a lifetime under half a second. The compound the team was after, seaborgium hexacarbonyl — Sg(CO)₆, one seaborgium atom dressed in six carbon monoxide ligands — had been synthesized once before, about ten years ago at the RIKEN Nishina Center in Japan. It was the first carbonyl compound ever made of a superheavy element, and its well-defined bonding made it the obvious target for the next question. But there is a decade-wide gap between making a molecule once and measuring how tightly it holds together.

To bridge it, the team ran gas chromatography on individual atoms. Seaborgium nuclei were thermalized in carbon monoxide gas behind the TASCA separator; the volatile hexacarbonyls drifted through a reactive-compound detector to a cooled adsorption array. And then the identifying trick: each atom was identified by its own radioactive decay. You don't weigh the sample. You wait for it to decay, and the decay tells you which atom just passed through.

In total, 75 seaborgium atoms were registered. Of those, exactly three formed Sg(CO)₆ — four percent. For comparison, the analogous experiment on tungsten, seaborgium's lighter group-mate, turned 17% of atoms into the hexacarbonyl. From that yield ratio, run through a reaction-rate model, the bond strength came out: the Sg–CO bond is 4 kJ/mol weaker than the W–CO bond (±2 kJ/mol on the difference). Three atoms, one bond, one number.

☠ How to kill Chapter 1

  • The indirect inference. Nobody held a ruler to the bond. The 4 kJ/mol came from a yield ratio — 3 of 75 versus 17% — read through a reaction-rate model. If that model misreads what actually happens on the cooled adsorption array, the number moves.
  • The volatility impostor. Gas chromatography measures how a molecule behaves on a surface. If the Sg(CO)₆ shortfall reflects adsorption quirks rather than bond strength, the experiment measures volatility and calls it chemistry.
  • Three atoms. The whole result stands on three atoms. A replication — more atoms, more runs — is the difference between a landmark and a footnote.
Chapter 2 · the trend that bent

The periodic table warps at its edge

Same paper. The claim that made the room sit up: relativistic effects reversing a group trend.

Down the chromium group, the hexacarbonyl bond gets stronger: chromium → molybdenum → tungsten. Seaborgium should have been the strongest of them all. It isn't.

The periodic table's deepest promise is that the pattern continues — that the empty rows below are just more of the same, waiting to be filled. The hexacarbonyls looked like a textbook case: from chromium to molybdenum to tungsten, the first bond dissociation energy climbs, and seaborgium, sitting directly under tungsten in group 6, was expected to extend the line upward. The derived values say otherwise: 188 kJ/mol for Sg(CO)₆ versus 192 kJ/mol for W(CO)₆. The trend reverses at element 106.

The cause is relativity, doing invisible work in the electron shells. Near a nucleus with 106 protons, the inner electrons are accelerated to a substantial fraction of light speed; they gain mass, their orbitals contract, and they shield the outer shells differently — reshaping the valence shells that do the chemistry. And here is the cleanest part: recent quantum-chemical calculations that fully include relativity reproduce the weakening, while calculations that neglect relativity predict the trend continues. One experiment, two theoretical frameworks, and reality cast the deciding vote.

☠ How to kill Chapter 2

  • The two-sigma wobble. The difference is 4 kJ/mol with an uncertainty of ±2 kJ/mol. It is real, but close enough to the noise floor that a single replication shifting the value could erase the reversal — watch for the next measurement.
  • The model dependency. "Relativity explains it" rests on quantum-chemical calculations agreeing with the data. If a non-relativistic treatment with better correlation handling can also reproduce 188 kJ/mol, the verdict softens.
Chapter 3 · the claim

State it so it can die

The pattern across the drawer, stated plainly enough to be wrong.

The claim: the periodic table is the most beautiful pattern in science — and at its far edge, it warps. Seaborgium's chemistry is not an extrapolation of tungsten's; relativity reshapes the valence shells, reverses the group trend, and hands reality the deciding vote between two theories. The elegance of the table is real, but it is not inert — it bends under its own weight.

Kin of this drawer, elsewhere in the cabinet: The Quantum Ladder — quantum effects climbing twenty-six orders of mass toward gravity. Both drawers live at edges: one at the edge of mass, this one at the edge of the table, where the certainty "the pattern continues" quietly breaks.

The rarity

75 atoms, 3 molecules, each atom announced by its decay. The measurement is the rarity: chemistry performed atom by atom, with a sample counted on fingers. The instrument — gas chromatography on individual nuclei — is the phenomenon, the way the sturgeon's spine was the phenomenon in the Part and the Job drawer.

The reversal

188 vs 192 kJ/mol, a 4 ± 2 kJ/mol reversal of the Cr→Mo→W trend. Relativity-on calculations agree; relativity-off calculations predict the trend continues. A clean experimental verdict on which framework describes reality.

The beauty as a signal

The table's elegance pointed at the deviation — the reversal only reads as a reversal against the beautiful pattern. Beauty as a signal again: this time it points at inner electrons accelerated near light speed, doing invisible work.

☠ How to kill the general claim

  • The next element. If bohrium or hassium compounds keep weakening in ways relativity models predicted all along, this drawer was a data point, not a warp — the pattern never broke, the models were just catching up.
  • The replication that flattens it. More atoms, tighter uncertainty: if the 4 kJ/mol gap collapses inside the error bars, the table is unbent and the warp was measurement noise.
